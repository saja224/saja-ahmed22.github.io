Case study · Business case

Country Development & Economic Performance

A Power BI dashboard comparing 231 countries on economy, health and population, built to show the typical country, not just the giants.

  • Solo project
  • Power BI · Power Query · DAX
  • Economics & Development
  • 4 report pages

01 · The business question

One place to compare development across countries and regions

The scenario: an international economic research organization needs a single dashboard for senior management to:

  • Compare economic performance between countries and regions
  • Monitor health, population, trade and growth indicators
  • Support strategic policy and investment decisions

02 · The data

A country-indicators dataset, captured as one snapshot

263raw entities
231countries analyzed
44indicators in the raw file

The dataset has no dates. Country names that no longer exist, such as Serbia and Montenegro, suggest it was compiled before 2006, so the figures describe that period, not today.

03 · Preparation

Cleaned in Power Query, every step documented

  • Repaired the structure: a broken header row and columns read as text were fixed and re-typed.
  • Removed what isn’t a country: one aggregate (the European Union) and 31 near-empty territories, taking 263 entities to 231 countries.
  • Standardized and enriched: 15 country names corrected, and every country assigned to one of seven regions.
  • Kept missing values as “No Data”, never zero, so averages and rankings use only countries that report a value.

Validation: every output table holds 231 rows, with no duplicate countries and no country without a region.

04 · Model & analytical decisions

A star schema, and measures that describe reality

Geography is the hub: every topic table holds one row per country and joins it on Country, so one slicer filters the whole report.

$243B vs $16B

Median next to average

A few giant economies pull the average GDP far above the typical country, so the median sits beside it.

Weighted

Life expectancy of the average person

Weighted by population, not one vote per country, so large countries count as much as their people do.

No Data ≠ 0

Missing stays missing

Blank values are classified first, so a country with no data never lands in the lowest category.

// Share of world GDP — stays relative to the whole world, whatever the slicers
GDP Share % =
DIVIDE (
    [Total GDP],
    CALCULATE ( [Total GDP], REMOVEFILTERS () )
)

One of the report’s DAX measures. REMOVEFILTERS clears every filter in the denominator, so a country’s share is always measured against the world total.

05 · The dashboard

Three pages and a country profile

Executive Overview

KPIs, a GDP ranking with share of world GDP, and a world map by development level.

Health & Development

Life expectancy by region, birth versus death rates, and income against life expectancy for every country.

Economic Performance

Imports versus exports for the top traders, the fastest and slowest growth, and median inflation and growth by region.

Country Profile · drill-through

Open any country from another page to see its rank, key indicators, and how it compares with its region and the world.

06 · Insights

What the dashboard shows

  • 51%

    Wealth is concentrated

    The five largest economies hold 51% of world GDP. The average country has $243B of GDP; the median country only $16B.

  • 54 vs 78 yrs

    Income and health move together

    Life expectancy in low-income versus high-income countries. Infant mortality falls from 77 to 7 per 1,000 births.

  • 44 vs 72 yrs

    HIV explains the biggest exceptions

    Life expectancy where HIV prevalence is 5% or more, against below 1%. Botswana, Eswatini and South Africa sit far below what their income predicts.

  • 21 vs 2

    Population pressure sits in three regions

    Births minus deaths per 1,000 people in Sub-Saharan Africa, South Asia and the Middle East & North Africa, against Europe & Central Asia. Migration is not included.

Low income means GDP per capita below $2,000; high income, $15,000 or more. These are associations across countries in this dataset, not proven causes.

07 · Recommendations

What management should consider

  1. Target health investment where income is lowest

    Start where infant mortality exceeds 100 per 1,000 births, such as Angola, Afghanistan and Sierra Leone, and where HIV prevalence is 5% or more.

  2. Support stability in high-inflation economies

    31 of the 221 countries with data have inflation of 10% or more. Pair support with monitoring of external debt.

  3. Report medians and per-capita figures beside totals

    Totals are dominated by a few large economies. Show the median and the number of countries behind every indicator.

08 · Limitations

What this data cannot say

  • One undated snapshot, so no trends over time.
  • Findings are associations between countries, not proof of cause.
  • Regions and category cut-offs are my own analytical choices, not official classifications.
  • Some indicators are missing for more than half of the countries; averages use only countries with data.
